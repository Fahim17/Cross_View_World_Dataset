01 / Motivation

Geolocalization beyond the panorama.

Cross-view geolocalization finds where a ground image was captured by retrieving its matching satellite view—an important capability when GNSS is unavailable or unreliable.

Existing benchmarks are often restricted to a few cities and rely on information-rich 360° panoramas. Real cameras see less. CVW500k combines limited directional observations with global geographic diversity, creating a more realistic and substantially harder retrieval problem.

01

Viewpoint gap

Horizontal ground scenes and top-down satellite views exhibit extreme geometric and scale differences.

02

Partial overlap

A narrow ground view corresponds to only a small, unknown region of its satellite image.

03

Global ambiguity

Distant locations may share roads, vegetation, buildings, fields, and other repeating visual patterns.

06 / Analysis

Where it works.
Where it struggles.

Country-wise R@1 ranges from roughly 26% to 49% in dedicated regional experiments. Australia reaches 49.21% R@1, while Zambia reaches 93.96% R@10.

Known limitations

  • World-scale coverage is inherently sparse and geographically uneven.
  • Repeated roads, blocks, vegetation, and fields create visually plausible false matches.
  • GeoQueryNet prioritizes representational capacity over lightweight deployment.
